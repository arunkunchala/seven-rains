# SEVEN RAINS — Case One: "The Glasshouse"
### Story Bible v0.1 — Interactive Microdrama POC

---

## 1. The Pitch

A murder in a Bengaluru glass tower on the first night of the monsoon. The police have already picked their killer — a night-shift security guard with no money and no lawyer. The warrant executes on **Day 8**.

You are the only detective who thinks he's innocent. You have **seven days**, and each day gives you **twelve minutes**.

You don't fail this game by dying. You fail it by arriving at Day 7 not knowing enough to stop the wrong arrest.

---

## 2. Why Seven Days (the diegetic clock)

The seven-day limit is not an arbitrary game timer. It is written into the fiction:

- Anaya Sridhar dies in the early hours of **Monday**.
- The Sridhar family is politically connected and wants a fast, quiet close. Kalpa's investors want it quieter.
- The Commissioner's office signs off on a **provisional arrest warrant** for Salim Basheer, executable **Monday of the following week (Day 8, 09:00)**.
- Once executed, the case is administratively closed and transferred out of Crime Branch. Vidya loses access.

So the countdown is a bureaucratic guillotine, not a bomb. It creates pressure without melodrama, and it makes the player's goal *subtractive*: **disprove the wrong answer before it becomes the official one.** That's a stronger engine than a blank whodunit, because the player always has something concrete to argue against.

---

## 3. The Detective

**Inspector Vidya "Vee" Ramanathan**, 34. Crime Branch, Bengaluru City Police.

- Transferred in from Chennai eleven months ago. Everyone assumes she was pushed out. She was.
- Reads rooms before she reads files. Physically capable but not a fighter — she runs, climbs, and drives; she does not brawl.
- Her flaw, and the engine of the whole case: **she is drawn to the innocent-looking suspect and repelled by the guilty-looking one.** She over-corrects. Twice in this case she will be spectacularly right about who *didn't* do it and spectacularly wrong about who did.
- She's not a lone wolf. She has **Sub-Inspector Farhan Qureshi**, 27 — earnest, fast on a keyboard, terrible at lying — who runs records and CCTV from the station while she's in the field. He's the player's voice-in-the-ear during traversal beats and the reason exposition can be delivered while the player's thumbs are busy.

**Why she matters mechanically:** every gesture in the game is something Vee's body does. The player isn't operating a cursor; they're steadying her torch, turning her key, holding her tongue during an interrogation. Keeping the gesture vocabulary tied to one physical person is what stops 40 interactions per episode from feeling like a minigame compilation.

---

## 4. The Victim

**Anaya Sridhar**, 29. Co-founder and CTO of **Kalpa**, a climate-tech company whose flagship product is a carbon-capture measurement platform used by a dozen large industrial clients.

Found at 05:40 Monday at the base of the atrium stairwell in Kalpa's HQ — the "Glasshouse," a six-storey glass-and-steel building off Bellandur. Fall from the fourth-floor landing. The skylight above the atrium was leaking; the first real rain of the season had started at 03:20.

She was brilliant, exacting, and — this is the thing the player learns slowly — **not kind**. Everyone loved her from a distance. The people closest to her were the ones she used hardest.

**What the player thinks she was:** a whistleblower about to expose her CEO.
**What she actually was:** a whistleblower who had decided that exposing her CEO was worth destroying the 26-year-old she'd promised to protect.

## 6. The Solution

**Tanvi Rao killed Anaya Sridhar, and it was not premeditated.**

Three years ago Tanvi, then 23 and desperate to impress, built the carbon-capture model that became Kalpa's flagship. When the field data came back wrong, Reena Iyer told her to "smooth it." She did. It became the fraud.

Anaya discovered it and traced the authorship. On Sunday night she told Tanvi her plan: she would take it to the regulator, and the disclosure would name **Tanvi as sole author** of the falsified model. Kiran and Reena would be destroyed by association. Tanvi would be destroyed directly. Anaya's exact words, which Vee will hear reconstructed on Day 6:

> *"You're twenty-six. You'll survive it. They won't."*

They met on the fourth-floor landing at **03:41**. Tanvi grabbed Anaya's forearm to stop her walking away. Anaya wrenched free, over-balanced against a rail that had been repainted three days earlier and was still slick, and went over. She grabbed the **underside** of the rail on the way — hence the paint fleck under her left fingernails, and the broken nails on that hand only.

Tanvi went down the stairs. Anaya was already dead.

And then Tanvi did the cold thing — the thing that turns manslaughter into murder in the eyes of the story, if not the statute. She **staged it**: wiped the rail, moved Anaya's phone four metres away to break the timeline, took the yellow Kalpa-branded rain jacket she'd been wearing, and left through the **loading bay** at 03:47 — a door on the building-management circuit, not the badge system, and therefore invisible to every log the police pulled.

She did not know Salim had left his post. She did not plan for him to take the fall. By Day 4 she knows, and she says nothing. **That silence is her real crime, and it's what the finale is about.**

## 7. The Clue Chain (7 days, fair-play audit)

Every clue below is *physically present* and *player-obtainable*. The Fairness column notes the earliest episode a sufficiently observant player could have spotted it.

### Day 1 — "First Rain"
The scene. Everyone says fall; Vee says no.
- **C1 — The hands.** Broken nails on the left hand only; no bracing injuries on the palms. People who fall grab forward. She grabbed *up*.
- **C2 — The paint fleck.** A curl of grey enamel under the left nails. Unidentified.
- **C3 — The dry phone.** Anaya's phone lies 4m from her hand, screen-up, face clean — but the skylight was leaking from 03:20. Something moved it after the rain started.
- **Sting:** Farhan calls. The badge logs show exactly one person in the building. *Salim Basheer.*

### Day 2 — "One Name on the List"
The logs, the building, the accused.
- **C4 — Badge logs are incomplete.** The loading bay runs on the BMS circuit, not the access system. It doesn't appear on any log the station pulled. *(Seeded here, paid off Day 5.)*
- **C5 — Salim's account has a 41-minute hole**, and he's lying about it badly.
- **C6 — Background detail:** on Vee's own scene photo of the fourth-floor pod, a **yellow rain jacket hangs on the hook at Tanvi's desk.** No dialogue draws attention to it. *(Fairness: obtainable Day 2. Paid off Day 6.)*
- **Sting:** the DCP tells Vee a warrant is being drafted.

### Day 3 — "Clean Numbers"
The fraud. The obvious motive. Peak red herring #1.
- **C7 — The falsified dataset**, recovered from Anaya's laptop; a regulator email drafted, unsent.
- **C8 — Kiran's motive is total** — and his alibi is airtight and *verified on screen*, which is unusual enough that a genre-literate player should get suspicious of the whole framing.
- **C9 — The model's authorship metadata** is redacted in the copy Vee gets. Someone scrubbed the author field. *(Fairness: the question "who wrote it?" is now live, three days before the answer.)*
- **Sting:** Reena Iyer requests a lawyer before Vee has asked her anything.

### Day 4 — "The Boy in the Lobby"
Salim's lie breaks. Red herring #2 peaks.
- **C10 — The son.** The 41 minutes. Salim is innocent and has just made himself look worse.
- **C11 — Salim saw someone** through the rain-blurred atrium glass around 03:45: *a yellow rain jacket, moving fast, toward the loading bay.* He assumed it was PK, whom he'd seen in the parking level earlier.
- **C12 — PK's debts** and the siphoned money surface. He's loud, he's lying, he's in the building. He's a thief.
- **Sting:** PK is picked up. For about eleven minutes it looks solved. Then his parking-level timestamp clears him.

### Day 5 — "Ninety Seconds"
Devika Nair. The turn.
- **C13 — The HVAC log.** The atrium's building-management system recorded a **loading bay door event at 03:47**. No badge. No name.
- **C14 — The camera clock is 90 seconds fast.** The atrium camera was never re-synced after a power event. Every timeline the case is built on is misaligned. Realigned, the footage puts **a second figure** in the atrium after Anaya's fall.
- **Sting:** the second figure is too blurred to identify — except for a pale shape at the shoulder.

### Day 6 — "Underside"
The physical proof, and the human one.
- **C15 — The paint fleck matches the rail's underside**, repainted the previous Thursday by facilities. Anaya grabbed *upward and under* — consistent with being pulled off balance and falling backward, not stepping over.
- **C16 — The jacket.** Kalpa issued yellow rain jackets at the last offsite. Cross-referenced against the Day 2 scene photo, one is missing from its hook — **Tanvi's**.
- **C17 — Anaya's unsent draft**, recovered from a synced tablet, naming the sole author of the falsified model: *T. Rao.* Motive lands.
- **Sting:** Tanvi has requested compassionate leave and a flight is booked for tomorrow morning.

### Day 7 — "Seven Rains"
The accusation. See §9.

## 8. Why the twist should land

The reveal has to recontextualise, not just surprise. Three things pay off backwards:

1. **Tanvi is present in every episode**, always helpful, always volunteering — because she needs to know what Vee knows. On replay, every one of her helpful moments reads as surveillance.
2. **The player's own bias is the trap.** Vee is drawn to sympathetic suspects. So is the player. Tanvi is the grieving junior; the game repeatedly gives the player the chance to be gentle with her, and every gentle choice is a missed tell.
3. **The moral inversion.** The player spends six days trying to save an innocent man. On Day 7 they succeed by destroying a woman who is guilty of one bad second and four days of silence. Vee gets her win and doesn't want it. That ambivalence is the reason to come back for Case Two.

---

## 9. Day 7 — The Accusation (structure)

Day 7 breaks the episode template. It has **two clocks**: the 12-minute real timer and the warrant executing at 09:00 in-fiction.

- **Act 1 (0:00–4:00):** Airport road. Traversal set piece — Vee has to intercept Tanvi before the flight. Highest interaction density of the series.
- **Act 2 (4:00–8:30):** The interrogation. Entirely Class D gestures — interrupt, hold, pressure. Tanvi does not crack under aggression; she cracks when Vee stops pushing. **The winning move is restraint** — a beat where the correct input is *no input for six seconds.* The series has been teaching this since Episode 3.
- **Act 3 (8:30–12:00):** The Case Board. The player must chain **five evidence cards** into a causal argument in front of the DCP before the warrant executes. This is where accumulated Clean-tier clues cash out.

### Endings (determined by cards held, not by a choice menu)

| Cards | Ending | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0–7 | **Warrant Executes** | Salim is arrested. Vee is removed from the case. Tanvi flies out. The player sees the true reconstruction as a cold epilogue they had no power to change. |
| 8–12 | **Reasonable Doubt** | The warrant is stayed. Salim goes home. Nobody is charged for Anaya. Unsatisfying on purpose. |
| 13–17 | **The Case Closes** | Tanvi is charged. Salim is cleared. Kiran and Reena walk on the fraud. |
| 18+ (7/7 streak) | **Seven Rains** | Full closure, including the fraud referral — and the private scene between Vee and Tanvi in the corridor afterwards that reframes the whole week. **Streak-gated.** |

The player never picks the killer from a list. They either built the argument or they didn't. This is the single most important design decision in the case: **the accusation is an assembly task, not a multiple-choice question.**

---

## 10. Series Continuity

Case One ends with a thread for Case Two: the fraud referral goes to a regulator whose file number Vee recognises — it's the same case number that ended her career in Chennai.

Each case is a self-contained week. The hook is that Vee's Chennai case is the through-line across cases, revealed one file at a time.
