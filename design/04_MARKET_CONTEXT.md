# Microdrama & Interactive Drama — Market Context
### Research briefing, September 2026

Compiled from ~50 searches and page fetches. Claims are attributed; where sources conflict or a figure could not be verified, it says so. **Read §6 before anything else if you only read one section.**

A note on units: Chinese sources report in 亿 (100 million) and English aggregators routinely mangle this by 10–100×. Figures below are unit-corrected where verifiable.

---

## 1. The format is real, large, and its growth has already broken

### Size
- **China 2024: ¥50.4B**, which **exceeded mainland box office (~¥42.5B)** for the first time ([US Dept of Commerce](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/china-entertainment-micro-drama-industry)). 2025: ¥63.4B on the narrow definition, ¥100B on DataEye's total-output basis — roughly **2× box office** ([36Kr / DataEye](https://36kr.com/p/3648642936500739)).
- **Ex-China 2025: $2.7B → 2026: $3.6B → 2031: $9.5B** (Media Partners Asia). US alone **$1.5B in 2026**.
- **696 million users in China** as of June 2025, ~70% of netizens. **US MAU 66M in 2025**, up from 26M in 2024 ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/hollywood-latest-mobile-play-bingeable-vertical-dramas)).

### The break
This is the most important thing in the entire briefing.

| | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Global downloads | 370M (+520% YoY) | 850M+ (+140% YoY) |
| Global app IAP revenue | ~$700M (~4× YoY) | ~$750M (**+20% YoY**) |

Revenue growth collapsed from ~300% to ~20% in twelve months while installs still grew 140%. Q1 2026 installs came **77% from SE Asia, LatAm and India** — the three lowest-ARPU regions. Naavik measured US **ARPDAU falling from >$2.50 to well under $0.50**, and calls it a *"leaky bucket dynamic"* ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/the-microdrama-volume-vs-value-paradox/)).

In China the leading indicator has already turned: DataEye's UA-spend proxy fell **16% H1→H2 2025**, and DataEye's own verdict is that the sector moved from 爆发期 (explosive growth) to **存量竞争期 — zero-sum "stock competition."**

### Retention is the disease
| Metric | Short drama | Other Entertainment apps |
|---|---|---|
| Install → first payment @35 days | **5.5%** | 1.6% |
| Revenue per install @14 days | **$0.83** | $0.15 |
| First-month ARPU (payers) | **$23.85** | $13.09 |
| **6-month retention** | **1.8%** | 8.0% |

([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/short-drama-mobile-acquisition-monetization)). Naavik's app-level curve: **D1 ~27%, D7 <10%, D30 ~2%, D90 ~1%.**

Microdrama monetises ~5.5× faster than other entertainment and retains ~4.4× worse. **It is a burn-and-churn extraction model, not a subscription business.**

### The cost that kills it
- **ReelShort's UA = 55% of revenue** (2025). In China, buying traffic is **80–90% of total cost**; production is 10–30%.
- **US marketing spend runs ~9× production cost** ([Digital Content Next](https://digitalcontentnext.org/blog/2026/03/05/how-microdramas-hook-viewers-and-drive-revenue/)). Jen Cooper: *"Marketing spend is around nine times the production costs."*
- ~90% of individual titles lose money in China. 80–90% of overseas projects can't break even.

**Why this matters more than anything else here:** every structural problem in the microdrama business is an *acquisition* problem. We are not building an app. We already own the audience. Our CAC for this event is zero, and the single worst line item in the entire industry does not apply to us. The thing microdrama is bad at — retention — is the thing a live game is built for.

## 2. The craft rules (these are specific and we should follow them)

### The Golden Three
Joey Jia, CEO of Crazy Maple Studios (ReelShort — the market leader), on the rule for the first three 90-second episodes: each must contain its own hook and cliffhanger.

> **"If you don't follow this specific formula, you will fail."**
> — [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/vertical-short-industry-hollywood-reelshort-dramabox-1235009933/)

### The funnel, and where it leaks
- **~30% never click through** if not hooked in **3 seconds**
- **60%+ drop during episodes 1–3**
- At the first paywall (ep 7–15), **90%+ exit immediately**
- ([Asia TV Forum](https://www.asiatvforum.com/en-gb/blog/industry-insights/beyond-views-the-critical-microdrama-metrics-the-industry-still-isnt-measuring.html) — the best funnel dataset available, and also the least formally attributed. Treat directionally; it is consistent with RevenueCat's 5.5% conversion.)

### Per-episode structure — producer-stated
From [InkTip](https://www.inktip.com/articles/writing-for-vertical-video-scripts) (producer-facing):
- *"The first five seconds matter most."* Drop into the middle of conflict. No set-ups.
- **One beat per episode** — a single clear turn.
- Every episode ends on one of exactly four cliffhanger types: **Revelation, Reversal, Deadline, Intrusion.**
- *"The vertical frame loves faces, hands, and intimacy. Two people arguing at a kitchen table works far better than a wide stadium shot."*

### The Chinese second-map
⚠️ This is circulating writers'-room doctrine ([163.com](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L30RCS1005340TH8.html)), not verified journalism — but it is strikingly consistent with the Tier-1 rules above.

| Window | Required beat |
|---|---|
| **0–3s** | 黄金钩子 "golden hook" — direct confrontation, hard suspense, or extreme status contrast |
| **0–10s** | Establish core conflict, character relationships, protagonist's immediate objective |
| **0–30s** | First reversal / **爽点** — emotional release after the opening tension |
| **every 20–30s** | An emotional checkpoint: dialogue clash, action beat, or reveal |
| **final 5–10s** | Cliffhanger — mystery hook, crisis hook, or reversal hook |

Named failure modes: exposition past 10 seconds; a flat ending with no hook; **sustained suffering (虐) with no counterbalancing reversal (爽)**.

### The scoring lexicon
Chinese writers' rooms score scripts against a checklist of 点 ([The World of Chinese](https://www.theworldofchinese.com/2025/09/welcome-to-the-micro-drama-nation-china/)):

**爽点** satisfaction/catharsis · **燃点** ignition/hype · **笑点** laugh · **泪点** tear · **磕点** shipping/squee · **爆点** blow-up

Stated audience goal: **身心舒畅** — total physical and mental satisfaction.

### What the tropes are *for*
The load-bearing insight is about **compression of justice**. Face-slapping (扇大嘴巴子), public kneeling apologies, rebirth-revenge (重生复仇): the humiliation is staged publicly, the reversal is witnessed by the humiliators, and **the payoff lands inside the same 90 seconds.** Long-form TV defers this for a season. Microdrama pays it out every episode. **That cadence is the product.**

Rebirth/revenge specifically works because it is *pure agency restoration* — you know who wronged you and you can pre-empt them.

### Vertical framing
Depth, not width. Close-ups and mediums are the whole vocabulary; wides are useless. Characters enter bottom, exit top. Two-shots staged in **depth layers**, not side by side. Dialogue cut as reaction ping-pong between singles ([Script Gods](https://www.scriptgodsmustdie.com/verticals-part-2-shooting-them/)). Format and budget are aligned — the cheapest thing to shoot is also the right thing to shoot. This is a large part of why microdrama works and Quibi didn't.

### Production reality
- **$100–300K per series**, 5–10 day shoots, **12–20 script pages/day** (vs 4–6 for a sitcom), 50–100 episodes, mostly non-union. AI-accelerated: **≤$60K, 3–4 weeks.**
- Reference: a US half-hour sitcom is **$2–4M per episode**.
- ReelShort runs ~20 in-house writers producing 15–20 scripts a month. Jia admits plots are *"generated algorithmically, based on what has previously performed well."*

## 3. Viewer psychology

**The base motivation is decompression, not story.** In a ZMET study of 36 heavy-viewing Chinese Gen X women, *"decompress" appeared in all 36 responses* ([Frontiers in Psychology, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1862137/full)). *"Only when I watch short dramas can I temporarily let go of all the pressure."*

**Cognitive gratification is inverted.** Unlike traditional media, microdrama doesn't satisfy information hunger — it *relieves fatigue from information overload* by permitting mental disengagement, delivering gratification *"without exerting excessive cognitive effort"* ([Jiao 2025, Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126018969.pdf)).

**Flow is the proximal addiction mechanism.** Content features → escapism motivation → flow → addictive use. Model explains **43.2% of variance** in addictive use ([Frontiers, n=658 adults 60+](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1815140/full)).

**They're paying for impatience, not content.** Naavik: *"The app monetizes impatience rather than content alone."* Jiao: the deliberately slow climb to the climax *"intensifies the inability to resist paid early access."* **The frustration is the monetisation engine.**

**Who watches:** core Western audience women 25–55; ReelShort's US primary is reported as single mothers 40–50; CandyJar reports 99.9% female. Male share is rising fast at some apps. China skews older and is ageing further — 55+ went from 27.5% to 33.3% of the audience in a year. **70% watch in bed before sleeping.**

**The competitive frame, from an operator:** Osher Assouline, AppReel co-CEO — ***"We are not competing with TV. We are competing with TikTok and games."*** That is the single most clarifying quote in this file, and it's the one that puts our event in the right frame.

**Engagement is genuinely excellent.** Short drama averages **25 min/day globally** (April 2026), up 85% from Jan 2025. Q4 2025 US mobile daily minutes: **ReelShort 35.7 vs Netflix 24.8, Prime Video 26.9, Disney+ 23.0** ([Omdia](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260222198738/en/Microdramas-Overtake-Streamers-on-Mobile-Engagement-Says-Omdia)). Omdia's framing: *"winning the battle for attention, rather than scale."*

## 4. Interactive drama: the graveyard, read carefully

### Netflix killed all of it — and the reason is not what people assume
- **Dec 2024:** ~20 of 24 interactive titles delisted. **12 May 2025:** the last two (*Bandersnatch*, *Kimmy Schmidt*) removed. **Netflix now has zero interactive titles.**
- Stated reason, verbatim: *"The technology served its purpose, but is now limiting as we focus on technological efforts in other areas."* And from gaming leadership: *"the technology was very limiting and the potential for what we could do in that realm was kind of capped."*
- ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/entertainment/netflix-is-delisting-all-but-4-of-its-interactive-shows-and-movies/), [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/digital/news/black-mirror-bandersnatch-removal-netflix-1236392097/))

**Read that precisely.** The cause was *not* audience rejection and *not* production cost. It was that a bespoke branching player had to be maintained across every TV, phone, browser and set-top box Netflix supports, for 24 titles out of thousands. **Interactive video was a client-engineering tax on the whole platform.** This is the most under-appreciated failure mode in the category — and it is the one that does not apply to us, because we own our client.

### The Chinese 2019–21 wave died of branch cost
iQiyi, Tencent, Youku and Bilibili all shipped interactive drama in 2019 (declared 互动剧元年). *Bandersnatch* shot **150 minutes of unique footage** so a viewer could watch **40–90**. Roughly 40–70% of production spend is content any given viewer never sees, with no mechanism to charge for it. The GameRes post-mortem on 《隐形守护者》 names the trap exactly: **film's cost base, with neither industry's business model.**

### ByteDance's 2023–24 attempt — the cleanest failure on record
Three interactive drama mini-programs (泡泡心选, 爪爪心选, 果冻心选), **all shut down 17 June 2024**, under a year from launch. Flagship title got **under six figures of plays**. Pricing: **¥199/month** against a market trained on **¥0.8–1 per episode** — a ~200× premium for a format nobody asked for. 36Kr's conclusion: a format that changes *the way you watch* being rejected is entirely normal ([36Kr](https://36kr.com/p/2835132832723209)).

### ⭐ The Crazy Maple experiment — the most decision-relevant fact in this document
The same company, the same founder, the same IP library, the same audience:

| | **Chapters** (interactive fiction, 2017) | **ReelShort** (linear microdrama, 2022) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$17–21M/yr US run-rate, **declining** | **$785M (2025)**, ~$1.05B projected 2026 |
| Trajectory | Down QoQ on revenue and downloads | First profit expected 2026 |

Joey Jia's own account: he founded Crazy Maple as *"an IP monetization platform,"* built Chapters, then in 2022 decided to *"leverage Chapters' IP, and bring this new form of content to the States"* ([TIME](https://time.com/7173765/reelshort-crazy-maple-studio-joey-jia-interview/)).

**He kept the IP, the audience and the emotional-beat template — and dropped the interactivity. Roughly 40–60× more revenue from the linear version.**

They did come back to it: **ReelShort Interactives** launched quietly in late 2024. But as a garnish on a product already at $785M — not as the core. **The sequencing is the lesson.**

### The whole interactive-fiction category is shrinking
Sensor Tower, US, Q1 2025 — every title declined QoQ on both revenue and downloads except Love and Deepspace: Chapters $408K→$329K/wk, Episode $312K→$223K, Love Island $276K→$177K. Pixelberry (Choices, $544M lifetime) was laid off in Jan 2024 and again in early 2026, and sold to an AI developer.

### ⚠️ THE TRAP — the false signal that has fooled five platforms in sequence
- Netflix Bandersnatch: **94% of viewers actively made choices**
- 《爱情公寓5》: **97.4% viewer participation**
- Mango TV 2026: **+30% average viewing time**

Every interactive product ever measured shows strong engagement *with the mechanic.* **Engagement with the mechanic is not evidence of a business.** If our pilot comes back with 90%+ interaction participation, that number is table stakes and predicts nothing. Netflix, iQiyi, Tencent, Youku and ByteDance were all fooled by it in turn.

## 5. But the counterexamples are real, and they have a shape

### The 2026 Chinese interactive revival — and the inversion that caused it
Interactive short drama is shipping in China right now at commercial scale:
- **Mango TV** 《马上来财》 (Spring Festival 2026): **330K peak daily visitors, 15M plays**, ~30% longer average viewing than comparable linear shorts
- **Fanqie/ByteDance** 《诡舍》 (March 2026): fully AI-generated cast, no live shoot, single-episode A/B choices that reconverge to the mainline
- **Pinduoduo**: interactive drama running inside 多多果园, where correct choices award in-game props — **explicitly not a content play**, the goal is session-time extension

The mechanic, per Tencent News (June 2026): *"选错分支直接触发BE…想改写剧情、解锁圆满结局只能充值回溯"* — **pick the wrong branch and you trigger a bad ending; to rewrite it you must top up to buy rewind attempts.**

**Understand what changed.** In 2019, a branch was a *cost* — footage most viewers never saw. In 2026, a branch is a *paywall* — the failure state **is** the monetisation event. Branching stopped being the expense and became the product. That inversion, not any technology, is why the category came back. (Enabled by AI cutting per-minute production from tens of thousands of RMB to **¥1,000–2,500** — 10–50×.)

Keep it in proportion: Mango's best number is a rounding error against Hongguo's hundreds of billions of cumulative views. **Interactive is a small early experiment inside a very large linear market.**

### Where interactive video demonstrably wins
1. **Detroit: Become Human — 15 million copies** as of Jan 2026, still selling seven years on ([Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2026/01/detroit-become-human-sales-top-15-million)). Cinematic branching drama driven by **gesture and QTE, not menus**. Rendered CG, premium purchase.
2. **Until Dawn / The Dark Pictures / The Quarry** — Supermassive built a business on episodic cinematic horror. (Though: 90 layoffs in Feb 2024, and the founders left in Jan 2024.)
3. **Sam Barlow — *Immortality*: 88 Metacritic, Edge 10/10** (the 24th in Edge's history). **Critically, his games do not branch.** The footage is fixed and finite; agency is over the *order of discovery*. Zero branching waste. This is the cheapest possible form of interactive video and it is the lineage that produced the awards.
4. **《隐形守护者》 — 1.4M copies**, #1 Chinese single-player game of 2019. Nobody imitated it: *"a case study recognised by capital, but too risky to imitate."*
5. **Love and Deepspace — $1B+ cumulative player spend, 50M players, no advertising at all.** Narrative sold as a **collectible with a deadline**, not consumed as a path. A branch you didn't take is a *purchase you missed*, not content that was wasted.
6. **Erica's Jack Attridge**, on the design rule: the player must be given something to do **every 15–20 seconds** or they disengage. And on why live action: *"The thing that's really expensive in the game is trying to render something close to a human face and an emotion. That's the thing that's affordable in film."*

### The daily-cadence gap
Only two attempts at daily-cadence interactive video exist on record: **Netflix's *Trivia Quest*** (daily episodes for one month, April 2022, never renewed) and **Hongguo's 2026 daily check-in** — which is parasocial (actor voice calls, goodnight videos), not branching.

**Nobody has made daily-cadence interactive video work.** Note also that Hongguo — the largest short-drama platform in China, given a free hand to add "interactivity" — **chose parasocial mechanics over branching.** That is a data point, not an oversight.

A gap is ambiguous. It is either an opportunity or a graveyard, and this research cannot tell us which.

## 6. What this means for us — the six conclusions

**1. Our economics are inverted relative to the industry, and that is the whole opportunity.**
Microdrama's structural disease is that 55–90% of cost is acquisition and 6-month retention is 1.8%. We pay zero CAC and we're borrowing an audience that is already retained. We are not entering the microdrama business; we are importing microdrama's *craft* into a retention slot we already own. That framing should be in every deck.

**2. Do not build branches. Build hands.**
Every interactive-video corpse died of branch cost, platform tech debt, or the neither-film-nor-game problem. A gesture doesn't fork the video — everyone sees the same footage, so there is **zero branching waste**, which is the Sam Barlow economics applied to microdrama. And at one input every 15–20 seconds we are inside Attridge's flow threshold, whereas a choice every four minutes is exactly the sparseness that breaks both cinema's suture and a game's flow.

**3. The paywall is not our monetisation event, and we should be glad.**
The Chinese revival monetises by making you fail and pay to rewind. We can't and shouldn't — it would poison an in-game event. Our currency is **what the player knows and feels at the end of the week**, cashed out in the parent game's economy as cosmetics and status. That also keeps us clear of the 90%-exit-at-paywall cliff.

**4. Follow the craft rules literally, because they're the only part of this business that reliably works.**
3-second hook. Conflict, relationships and objective by 0:10. First 爽点 by 0:30. Emotional checkpoint every 20–30s. Cliffhanger in the final 5–10s. Faces, hands and intimacy in the frame. **A 12-minute session is eight ~90-second micro-episodes, each with its own hook and cliff — not one 12-minute film.** That last point is the Quibi-shaped mistake and it's the one we made.

**5. Measure the right thing from day one.**
Interaction participation will be 90%+. It means nothing. The metrics that matter: **D2 and D7 return within the event**, **completion of the 7-day arc**, **whether the event lifts parent-game DAU and session length on non-event days**, and **per-beat tier distribution** to find broken beats. If the event doesn't move parent-game retention, the drama was a hobby.

**6. Genre is our biggest unexamined risk.** See `05_V02_REDESIGN.md` §1.

## Caveats
- Genre-level quantitative data (which tropes convert, which are cooling) **does not exist in credible trackers**. Everything published on it is SEO content. The only hard datapoint: China H1 2026 themes — urban 36.8%, fantasy 28.7%.
- Market-size figures range from $2.7B to $26B depending on definition. Pick one and state it.
- The Asia TV Forum funnel numbers are the best available and the least formally attributed.
- Sensor Tower increasingly *undercounts* the market, since it excludes web payments — and web payments are exactly how ReelShort reached profitability.
